## Contact Orchestration

How many contacts to pursue per account, and which direction to work, keyed to the Company Size band above.

| Company size sub-band | Contacts to pursue | Direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 15–40 employees | 1–2 contacts | Top-down — start with Founder/CEO or COO/VP Operations (highest-priority personas above); a second contact only if the first goes cold |
| 40–100 employees | 2–3 contacts | Top-down — Founder/CEO or COO/VP Operations first, then layer in CFO or CIO/IT Director once the primary contact engages or stalls |

Direction is top-down because the star-priority table above already ranks Founder/CEO and COO/VP Operations highest — smaller accounts have fewer decision layers, so a bottom-up entry (e.g. Operations Manager) adds a hop without adding leverage. `email-writer`/`linkedin-dm` should sequence personas from this table once persona-driven branching (their Step 2 persona match) is in play, rather than contacting every persona simultaneously.



# Company-level
The company has 15–100 employees.
The company operates in Wholesale, Distribution, Import/Export, E-commerce, Light Manufacturing, or Consumer Goods.
The company is experiencing rapid growth, creating operational complexity.
The company relies on spreadsheets, manual processes, or disconnected business systems, OR already has an ERP that is slow and expensive to modify.
The company has visible operational bottlenecks affecting speed, visibility, or scalability.
The company is actively frustrated by the cost, speed, or trustworthiness of its current operational systems or implementation partner.
The company has hired or is hiring Operations Managers, ERP Administrators, Business Systems, or Systems Coordinators, signaling investment in operational maturity.
The company shows a recent growth trigger, such as hiring 10+ employees in the past 12 months, expansion, or new business initiatives.
The company is looking to improve operational efficiency rather than simply purchase new software.
The company has the budget and executive willingness to invest in operational transformation if ROI is clear.
The company values speed, visibility, and operational control over maintaining the status quo.
The company is not highly bureaucratic or resistant to adopting modern business systems.

The person has influence over ERP implementation or business systems decisions.
The person is actively seeking ways to reduce manual work and dependency on external consultants.
The person is measured on business outcomes such as operational efficiency, process execution, cost reduction, or company growth.
The person is likely to sponsor a discovery call if they believe it will uncover measurable operational improvements.
The person is frustrated by slow system changes, poor visibility, or operational inefficiencies and is motivated to solve them.
